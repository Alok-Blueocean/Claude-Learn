# CCAR-P: Official Scenario Model

**This exam guide does NOT use named scenarios.** Unlike the sibling Architect Foundations exam (CCAR-F), the CCAR-P exam guide (`CCAR-P_Architect_Professional_Exam_Guide.pdf`, Version 1.0) contains no set of named case studies, personas, or fictional companies that recur across multiple sample questions. There is no "Scenario A / Scenario B" structure and no appendix listing scenario-to-domain mappings anywhere in the guide's 16 sections.

What the guide provides instead, in Section 8 ("Sample Questions"), is three **standalone illustrative items**, each a short, self-contained situational stem followed by four answer options and a rationale. These are reproduced in full in `official_samples.md`. This document instead explains — using only what the guide itself says — how CCAR-P exam items are actually constructed, so you can calibrate your preparation correctly.

---

## How the guide says exam items are built

Pulling directly from the guide's own language across several sections:

**1. Items are written against task-statement objectives, not against scenarios.** Section 6 states: "Each domain below lists the tasks a candidate is expected to perform. Exam items are written against these objectives." The unit of item construction in this guide is the individual task-statement bullet under each of the seven domains (e.g., "Evaluate tool/agent configuration for capability bloat" under Domain 3) — not a shared scenario library. A given item is anchored to one or more of these bullets, drawing whatever situational detail it needs to test that specific task.

**2. Items are "illustrative" of style and cognitive level, not drawn from the live bank.** Section 8's own framing: "These illustrative items show the style and cognitive level of the exam. They are not drawn from the live item bank." This confirms the three sample items are representative of *format and difficulty*, not a preview of recurring scenario content.

**3. The stem pattern is short, situational, and single-decision.** All three published samples follow the same shape: a compact real-world situation (2–3 sentences) establishing a concrete system, constraint, or symptom, followed by a single decision point with four options (A–D), exactly one of which is correct given the stated constraints. There is no multi-part scenario that spans several questions, and no persona, company name, or running storyline carried between items — each sample stands entirely on its own.

**4. Scoring and reporting are domain-level, reinforcing that domains — not scenarios — are the exam's organizing unit.** Section 9 states the score report shows "the percentage of items you answered correctly within each content domain." Section 6's blueprint table is the only structural breakdown of the exam the guide provides. There is no scenario-level score breakdown because there is no scenario structure to break down.

**5. Preparation guidance points at the blueprint and the standalone samples, not at scenario study.** Section 7 ("How to Prepare") tells candidates to "Study the exam blueprint in Section 6 and self-assess against each objective" and "Complete the sample questions in Section 8 to familiarize yourself with item style" — again, blueprint objectives and item *style*, with no mention of scenario materials to study.

## What this means for your preparation

Because CCAR-P has no named scenarios to memorize, the most efficient prep strategy is:

1. **Master each domain's task-statement bullets individually** (reproduced in full in `tutorial.md`, Sections 3–9) — since every item traces back to one of these, not to a shared case study.
2. **Practice the single-decision, multi-constraint stem format** shown in the three official samples — a short situation, several plausible-looking options, and one option that is correct specifically because it best satisfies the *stated* constraint (least privilege, caching + latency + cost together, or a diagnostic first-step). The wrong options in all three official samples are not absurd — they're each individually reasonable-sounding but fail to address the specific constraint named in the stem. Practice questions in `practice_questions.md` and scenario Q&As in `scenario_qa.md` in this kit are deliberately built in this same style, at proportionally higher volume per domain weight.
3. **Do not expect recurring characters, companies, or a scenario index on the real exam** — if you encounter test-prep material elsewhere claiming CCAR-P has named scenarios (e.g., confusing it with CCAR-F), that material is not describing this exam guide.
